Deep Sky Gallery

Explore a collection of various nebula and galaxies captured with an intermediate telescope setup. Each image highlights the beauty and design of the cosmos through long exposure photography.

Emission Nebulae

Emission Nebulae are vast clouds of glowing gas and dust where stars are born. These vibrant colors reveal intricate shapes and wonderful detail when captured using narrowband filters.

Dark Nebulae

Dark Nebulae are dense clouds of dust that block out starlight behind them, creating silhouettes against the Milky Way. Some can be thick clumps of dust shrouding stars completely, whereas others can be much more diffuse and glow beautifully blue.

Galaxies

Galaxies are immense collections of billions of stars, along with dark lanes of dust, and mysterious dark matter all bound together by gravity. Many harbor supermassive black holes at their centers and hundreds of thousands of light years across.

About My Process

Astrophotography is a blend of technique, patience, and many long hours sitting outdoors. Each image begins long before the shutter opens and continues with post-processing for days or even weeks after. Here is a look at the workflow behind some of my best deep-sky photos featured on this site.

  1. Planning

    Planning starts several days in advance by reviewing weather forecasts, the moon phase, and selecting an idea imaging location. I choose my targets for the night based on the season and how well they fit my telescope's field of view, using tool like Astronomy Tools for FOV simulations. Stellarium is another app that helps me prepare by showing when my target rises, how high it will get, and where the moon will be throughout the night. Careful preparation ensures each imaging session produces the best data possible.

  2. Capture

    During capture, I use my telescope, a William Optics Redcat 51, an asi294MM dedicated astronomy camera, and various filters, all mounted on a guided tracking system to take long exposure images. Each session often lasts 2-6 hours, and many of my projects require multiple nights of imaging to reach the depth and amount of detail I am looking for. The mount tracks the motion of the night sky while the camera collects exposure after exposure, slowly building the data needed to reveal the faint structures of each target.

  3. Stacking

    Once each individual exposure is captured, I use Pixinsight to align and stack the frames into a single master image. Stacking increases the total integration time, improves resolution, and significantly reduces noise by combining the faint signal collected across many hours. This master file becomes the clean, high-quality foundation for the processing stage.

  4. Processing

    In the processing stage, I stretch the master image to reveal faint details, balance the colors, and adjust contrast to bring out the structure of the target. After refining the image in Pixinsight, I make final touch-ups in Photoshop and add an annotation for copyright. This step transforms the raw stacked data into the finished artwork you see in the gallery.

  5. Printing

    In the final stage, I bring the processed image into Lightroom, where I manage print settings and connect to my Canon printer. I print each image on metallic paper to enhance brightness and color depth, then mount it using matting boards for a polished finish. Every print is created in-house, allowing me to control the quality from start to finish.
